## 1. Product Overview

Campus Keke is a proposed campus transportation platform connecting
students with registered Keke drivers operating within Babcock
University. Beyond digital ride requests, the platform is designed
around the realities of campus transport: finding an available driver,
matching passengers travelling in compatible directions, managing
different vehicle capacities and giving drivers control over which
requests they accept. The product will be refined using evidence from
students, drivers and relevant campus stakeholders.

### 1.1 Product vision

To make movement around Babcock University more accessible and organised
by helping students find rides while helping drivers manage compatible
passenger requests and available seats.

### 1.2 Objectives

-   Reduce the need for students to physically search or signal
    repeatedly for an available Keke.

-   Help students in a hurry get a keke efficiently.

-   Give students clear booking confirmation and ride-status updates.

-   Help drivers manage requests, vehicle occupancy and passenger
    destinations.

-   Support direction-based shared rides so compatible passengers can
    use available seats without exceeding vehicle capacity.

-   Support private bookings that reserve the vehicle for one booking
    group.

-   Make the applicable fare and the driver's registered payment account
    details visible after a driver accepts, without processing payment
    inside the app.

## 2. Problem Definition

Initial student feedback indicates that finding a Keke can be difficult,
particularly when a student is in a hurry. The current process may
involve walking around or signalling drivers physically, with no clear
confirmation that a ride is secured. The team also wants to investigate
whether fixed-fare arrangements influence the destinations drivers
accept, whether vehicles travel with unused seats, and whether students
and drivers would find organised shared rides useful. These are problem
areas to validate through further research, not assumptions about every
student or driver.

## 4. Target Users

### 4.1 Students

Students are the intended passengers. Potential needs include requesting
a ride, selecting pickup and destination points, specifying passenger
count, choosing between shared and private rides, seeing whether a
driver has accepted, viewing the fare and accessing the accepted
driver's payment account details.

### 4.2 Keke drivers

Drivers are the service providers. Potential needs include registering
and verifying their identity and vehicle, setting availability,
receiving clear requests, seeing passenger count and destination
details, managing occupied and reserved seats, and accepting or
declining requests that fit their route and capacity.

### 4.3 Other stakeholders

Relevant campus representatives may need to clarify driver registration,
vehicle requirements, approved passenger capacities, permitted routes,
standard fares and whether extra charges for private or urgent rides are
allowed. The team should identify the appropriate authority rather than
assume these rules.

## 6.  User Stories

1. As a student, I want to request a Keke through the app so that I don’t have to stand by the roadside waiting or physically hail drivers.
2. As a student, I want to find available Keke drivers when I need a ride so that I can reduce the difficulty of getting picked up.
3. As a student, I want to enter my pickup and destination locations so that I can find a Keke driver who can take me where I need to go.
4. As a student, I want to see the expected fare before confirming a booking so that I can avoid unexpected payment demands from drivers.
5. As a student in a hurry, I want to request an urgent pickup so that I have a better chance of getting transportation when time is important.
6. As a student willing to pay for urgent transportation, I want to know the additional cost of an urgent pickup before booking so that I can decide whether the priority service is worth paying for.



### 7.1 Non-functional requirements

-   Usability: the booking process should be clear to both students and
    drivers.

-   Security: user accounts and personal information must be protected.

-   Reliability: booking requests and status changes should be recorded
    consistently.

-   Performance: common actions should respond within an acceptable
    time.

-   Data integrity: passenger, booking, vehicle-capacity and
    seat-reservation records must remain accurate, including when
    multiple requests are received at the same time.

## 8. Proposed Booking Workflow

Student: sign in → choose pickup and destination → enter passenger count
and luggage information → select shared or private ride → review fare →
submit request → receive driver response → view booking status and,
after acceptance, the driver's registered bank account details for
direct payment.

Driver: sign in → set vehicle category and availability → receive a
request or compatible shared-ride suggestion → review route, passenger
count, ride type, fare and available seats → accept or decline → update
the relevant passenger booking or journey status → complete the ride and
release the seats used by the passenger(s).

For shared rides, the system should suggest or present only requests
that appear compatible with the driver's current route and available
capacity. The driver retains final control over acceptance. A private
booking blocks additional pickups until it is completed. Each passenger
booking must be tracked separately so one passenger can alight without
ending the entire shared journey.

## 9. MVP Scope

The MVP should test the product's main difference from a basic
ride-booking app: matching passengers travelling in compatible
directions while managing vehicle capacity accurately. It should be
small enough to build and test with participating students and drivers,
but substantial enough to test whether shared rides and seat management
solve a real campus transport problem.

## 11. Success Measures and Risks

Possible measures for an MVP test:

-   Percentage of requests accepted by drivers.

-   Time between a request and driver response.

-   Time between acceptance and pickup.

-   Percentage of bookings completed.

-   Repeat use by students and continued participation by drivers.

-   Feedback from students and drivers after completed trips.

-   Percentage of shared-ride requests successfully matched with
    compatible passengers.

-   Number of attempted over-capacity bookings blocked by the system and
    confirmation that no overbooking occurs during testing.

-   Average number of occupied seats per completed shared journey, where
    this can be measured.

Key risks to investigate:

-   Students may prefer the existing way of finding Keke and may not
    adopt the app.

-   Drivers may not want to change their current work routine or may
    reject shared trips that create inconvenient detours.

-   Insufficient active drivers could leave requests unanswered.

-   Unclear fares, vehicle capacities, extra-charge rules or campus
    approval could prevent deployment.

-   Poor connectivity or inaccurate location data could affect booking
    reliability.

-   Incorrect seat-reservation logic could cause overbooking or prevent
    a driver from accepting a valid request.

-   Displaying incorrect or outdated bank account details could lead to
    failed or misdirected transfers; driver account details must be
    verified and updated securely.

-   Shared rides could create excessive waiting or detours if route
    compatibility is poorly defined.
